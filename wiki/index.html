    More Than Enough Wiki

    The game tells you nothing. Poke at it, find out, and come here when you want the details.

    MORE THAN ENOUGH is about turning a hostile wilderness into an absurd surplus. You start with nothing in an endless, randomly generated forest. Gather wood, stone, ore and crystal, craft better tools, put up buildings that work while you're away, dig into the rock below, and keep going until there's more than enough. Then start over in a new world, a little stronger.

    Controls

    Everything is reachable with one thumb. A keyboard works too.

    Moving

    Put a finger down anywhere on the map and drag: a virtual joystick appears under your thumb and follows it. Or tap a spot and you walk there in a straight line, stopping if something blocks the way.

    You walk at tiles per second, faster with better Boots and the Nomad perk, and slower in water.

    Gather

    The big round button in the bottom-right corner is Gather. Hold it to keep working, about four times a second. Each press does the most useful thing within reach:

    • If a building near you has goods waiting, it collects them (the button reads COLLECT).
    • Otherwise it works the nearest deposit you're standing among (the button reads GATHER +n, where n is what one swing yields). The tile it will work has a faint green outline.
    • With nothing in reach, the button dims and reads EXPLORE.

    You can also tap a tile directly: a resource within reach gets gathered, a building within reach gets collected. Reach is tiles from you to the tile's centre.

    Trails

    Hold Gather, then slide your thumb off the button onto the map and draw. Every resource tile within reach that your stroke passes over lights up green. Let go and each one is gathered once. It's the fastest way to clear a patch around you. Underground the same stroke digs, and glows amber.

    A tile counts as crossed when its centre is within tiles of your stroke.

    Zoom

    Use the − / + buttons above the thumb buttons, pinch with two fingers, or scroll a mouse wheel. You can zoom in to 3× and out until tiles are just a few pixels wide.

    Tilt

    The view can tilt from straight down to an angled, 2.5D look, where things have height. Mountains rise as cliffs, water sits below the bank, and underground the rock you haven't dug stands a full layer tall around your tunnels.

    • The tilt button (the slanted square beside the zoom buttons) swings between flat and fully tilted.
    • On touch, slide two fingers up or down together to tilt by any amount. Spreading them still zooms.
    • On a computer, press T, or hold Shift and scroll.

    Tilted, a tap picks whatever block you can see at that spot, so tapping the top of a wall digs that wall. If a wall stands in front of you, you show through it faintly. Your zoom and tilt are remembered in this browser.

    The other buttons

    • BUILD opens the sheet on the Build tab. See Buildings.
    • UPGRADE opens the sheet on the Upgrades tab. See Upgrades.
    • MINE digs a hole where you stand and drops you into the layer below; underground it becomes UP, which climbs to the layer above. It dims where you can't. See The mine.

    Close the sheet with Done, or swipe it down by its handle.

    The screen

    There's no text to read in the game. Here's what everything you see means.

    Resource counters

    The four chips at the top are what you're carrying: 🪵 wood, 🪨 stone, ⛏️ ore and 💠 crystal. A green +n/s under a count is your income from buildings and the Abundance Engine, assuming you're there to pick it up.

    Depth marker

    Underground, a small ▼ n marker under the counters shows which layer you're on: 1 is just below the forest.

    On the map

    • Faint green outline: the tile Gather will work next.
    • Blue outlines (while the Build tab is open): where each kind of building would go.
    • A ring around a building fills green as goods pile up and turns gold when it's full. A full building stops working until you collect.
    • Little sprites flying to you: resources heading into your packs.
    • A golden burst of sparks and a fanfare: you've reached a milestone.
    • Darkness after dusk, with a pool of lamplight around you. See The world.
    • Black: fog. You haven't been there yet.

    The sheet

    BUILD and UPGRADE open a sheet with three tabs: Upgrades, Build and Beyond. Prices show in green when you can afford them and red when you can't. In Upgrades, each family has a row of dots: an empty dot hasn't been started, a tinted dot is affordable now, a half dot is part way, and a solid green dot is maxed.

    The bottom of the sheet holds Wiki (this page), Reset world (starts over, after asking), and Sound.

    Performance panel

    Tap the version badge in the top corner (or press F3) to see how well the game runs on your device, phone or computer alike. Tap it again to hide it. Its left edge is green at 55 fps or more, yellow from 30, red below. It stays open next time if you leave it open.

    If something breaks

    If the game hits an error it stops and shows a red The game broke panel saying what went wrong, with a Reload button. If saving stops working (storage full or blocked), a message says so straight away.

    The world

    Endless in every direction, different every time, and it goes down as well as out.

    Layout

    Each world comes from a random seed and has no edges: keep walking and it keeps going. Resources are spread evenly everywhere with no richer zones far from the start, so every direction is worth exploring. You begin on the dry land nearest the world's centre.

    The world has layers. The forest you start in is the surface; the mine is a stack of layers directly beneath it, lined up tile for tile. See The mine.

    Surface tiles

    Richness is how many swings a deposit takes before it's exhausted. Each tile rolls its own value in that range.

    Fog

    The surface starts black. Everything within tiles of you is revealed as you walk, and stays revealed. The Cartographer perk widens this by 2. The mine has no fog.

    Water

    You can swim anywhere there's water, at of your walking speed. You'll be riding a little boat. Nothing can be built on water.

    Regrowth

    Forest and stone grow back; ore and crystal never do. Every seconds, each depleted forest or stone tile within tiles of you has a chance to regain one point of richness, up to where it started. Tiles far from you don't regrow, so a cleared patch only comes back while you're nearby.

    Day and night

    A full day lasts seconds and a new world starts at noon. Days are bright and warm. After dusk the world fades into darkness, except for a pool of lamplight around you, so you can always keep working. Nothing else changes at night.

    Gathering

    Four resources, two tools, and deposits that run dry.

    Resources

    • 🪵 Wood from forest, and from fossils in the deep mine. Uses the Axe.
    • 🪨 Stone from stone deposits and stone seams. Uses the Pickaxe.
    • ⛏️ Ore from ore deposits and ore seams. Uses the Pickaxe. Never regrows on the surface.
    • 💠 Crystal from crystal deposits and, from layer 3 down, crystal seams. Uses the Pickaxe. Rare, and never regrows on the surface.

    Yield and richness

    Each swing gives you as much as your tool's level allows (1 at the start, up to 32), but always takes exactly one point of richness from the tile. Better tools don't use up deposits faster; they get more out of each one. A forest at zero richness turns into a stump until it regrows.

    See Upgrades for the full tool tables.

    Finding ore and crystal

    Ore and crystal show up as rocks flecked with orange, and as pale blue crystal clusters. They're scattered everywhere, but crystal is rare. If none is near, walk in a straight line; the fog hides a lot.

    The mine

    Solid rock under the forest, as deep as you care to dig.

    Holes: going down and back up

    Every way between layers is a hole you can see, so it's always clear where you went deeper and where you came back up.

    • MINE, in the forest, digs a hole at your feet and drops you into a one-tile chamber in the rock directly below. It works on open ground, not in water.
    • UP, underground, climbs to the layer above through the hole you came down, or digs a new one through the ceiling where you stand. You can't dig up through bedrock, or come up inside a mountain.
    • From above, a hole is a dark pit in the floor; tap it within reach to drop through. From below, it's a pool of light falling through the ceiling, and a beam when the view is tilted.
    • Holes stacked on top of each other make a column: drop into the top one and you fall all the way to the bottom.
    • You can't gather from or build on a tile with a hole in it.

    Seeing in the dark

    Each layer starts unexplored, just like the forest: unseen rock is dark and reads as solid. Underground you see the rock within tiles of you, and it stays revealed. Shafts are hidden until you get close.

    Tilt the view (see Controls) and the world is cut open a few rows in front of you, showing every layer as a band on the cut: what you've explored is lit, layers you've been down to are dim, and layers nobody has reached are dark, all the way down. Underground, the layers above you show faintly over the cut, up to the forest floor. A bright gap between two bands is a hole.

    Digging

    Underground, everything is solid and you can only walk through tunnels you dig. Dig a tile by tapping it within reach, or with a Trail across nearby rock. Holding Gather on its own never digs, so you never carve tunnels by accident.

    Each dig clears one tile into tunnel. Seams pay out like surface deposits, using your Axe or Pickaxe level. Tunnels stay where you dug them, on every layer.

    What's down there

    Deeper layers hold more kinds of rock:

    Shafts

    Every × area of each layer hides exactly one shaft, a marked hatch at least two tiles in from the area's edge. Dig your way to it and dig it once to open it. Opening it doesn't drop you; when you're ready, tap the open shaft from within reach and you fall to the layer below, landing directly underneath it. Shafts are the only way deeper than you've been.

    Layers line up exactly: the tile at a given spot on layer 3 is directly below the same spot on layer 2 and on the forest.

    Rules underground

    • Nothing can be built underground.
    • Logistics only collects from buildings on the layer you're on, so your surface camps simply fill up while you're below.
    • Nothing regrows underground.
    • Day and night carry on as usual.

    Buildings

    Make the world work for you.

    Placing

    Open BUILD, stand next to the right kind of tile, and tap Place. The building goes on the nearest suitable free tile within reach; blue outlines on the map show where. Each building you already own of a kind makes the next one of that kind cost × more.

    Extractors

    Extractors stand on a matching deposit and produce its resource forever. They don't use the deposit up.

    Converters

    Converters make a resource out of nothing on any open land: grass or any surface deposit, but not water or mountain. Each needs its blueprint from the Unlocks family in Upgrades first.

    Output

    A building's real rate is its base rate multiplied by Overclock, its resource's refinery chain, the Abundance Engine and the Legacy perk. The Build tab shows the current figure for each.

    Goods pile up inside the building until it's full ("Holds" above), then it stops. Nothing is lost; it just waits.

    Collecting

    Stand within tiles of a building and press Gather (it reads COLLECT) or tap the building. You take every whole unit from every building in that range at once.

    Logistics

    Once you unlock Logistics, every building within tiles of you sends its goods straight into your packs as they're made. Walk away and they fill up again. The trick is to cluster buildings into a base and work from the middle of it.

    Upgrades

    Everything in the Upgrades tab, level by level.

    Tools

    Your Axe and Pickaxe set how much each swing yields. Boots set your speed. Overclock multiplies every building's output.

    Unlocks and blueprints

    One-time purchases.

    Refineries

    Each resource has a chain of ten refinery levels. Every level multiplies all production of that resource, from extractors and converters alike, and the levels compound.

    Beyond

    Start over in a new world, a little stronger each time.

    Embarking

    Once you've taken in resources in your current world, the Embark button in the Beyond tab lights up. Everything that reaches your packs counts: gathering, digging, collecting, Logistics and the Abundance trickle.

    Embarking throws you into a brand new random world. You lose the old world, your inventory, tools, buildings, unlocks, refineries, the Abundance Engine and your milestone progress. You keep your Insight, your perks and your lifetime total.

    Insight

    Each Embark pays out Insight based on everything you've ever gathered, across all worlds:

    Insight gained = floor(√(lifetime total ÷ 2,000))

    So 20,000 lifetime gives 3, 80,000 gives 6, and 200,000 gives 10. Insight is spent on perks and never resets. The requirement to embark only counts the current world, so you can't embark again right away on old progress.

    Perks

    Legacy multiplies all building output and the Abundance trickle by 1.25 per level, compounding. Memory, Heirloom and Cartographer take effect from the next world you start.

    Milestones

    The game quietly tracks these in order and celebrates each one with a burst of sparks and a fanfare. Spoilers, if you'd rather find your own way.

    Milestones are checked in order, so one only counts once everything before it is done. They start over when you embark.

    Saving

    Automatic, and kept in your browser.

    • The game saves every few seconds, right after anything important, and when you leave the page.
    • Saves live in your browser's local storage on this site. They don't follow you to another browser or device, and a private or incognito window may forget them when it closes.
    • Clearing site data for the game deletes your save.
    • Reset world at the bottom of the sheet starts a fresh world. It asks first, and it can't be undone.
    • While the game is in active development, a new version may not read older saves. If a save can't be read, the game tells you and starts a new world.
