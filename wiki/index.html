    More Than Enough Wiki

    The game tells you nothing. Poke at it, find out, and come here when you want the details.

    ▶ Play now, free, in your browser

    MORE THAN ENOUGH is about turning a hostile wilderness into an absurd surplus. You start with nothing in an endless, randomly generated forest. Gather wood, stone, ore and crystal, craft better tools, put up buildings that work while you're away, dig into the rock below, and keep going until there's more than enough. Then start over in a new world, a little stronger.

    Controls

    Everything is reachable with one thumb. A keyboard works too.

    Moving

    Put a finger down anywhere on the map and drag: a virtual joystick appears under your thumb and follows it. Or tap a spot and you walk there in a straight line, stopping if something blocks the way.

    You walk at tiles per second, faster with better Boots and the Nomad perk, and slower in water.

    Gather

    The big round button in the bottom-right corner is Gather. Hold it to keep working, about four times a second. Each press does the most useful thing within reach:

    • If a building near you has goods waiting, it collects them (the button reads COLLECT).
    • Otherwise it works the nearest deposit you're standing among (the button reads GATHER +n, where n is what one swing yields). The tile it will work has a faint green outline.
    • With nothing in reach, the button dims and reads EXPLORE.

    You can also tap a tile directly: a resource within reach gets gathered, a building within reach gets collected. Reach is tiles from you to the tile's centre.

    Trails

    Hold Gather, then slide your thumb off the button onto the map and draw. Every resource tile within reach that your stroke passes over lights up green. Let go and each one is gathered once. It's the fastest way to clear a patch around you. Underground the same stroke digs, and glows amber.

    A tile counts as crossed when its centre is within tiles of your stroke.

    Zoom

    Use the − / + buttons above the thumb buttons, pinch with two fingers, or scroll a mouse wheel. You can zoom in to 3× and out until tiles are just a few pixels wide. Between the buttons, beside a magnifier, is how far you're zoomed (1× is normal, 2× twice as close); tap it to go back to 1×.

    Tilt

    The view always leans a little, and can tilt further to an angled, 2.5D look where things have height. Mountains rise as cliffs, water sits below the bank, and underground the rock you haven't dug stands a full layer tall around your tunnels. A layer is one and a half tiles high, a little taller than the wanderer, so a tunnel is somewhere you could walk.

    • The tilt button (the slanted square beside the zoom buttons) swings between the slight lean and fully tilted.
    • On touch, slide two fingers up or down together to tilt by any amount. Spreading them still zooms.
    • On a computer, press T, or hold Shift and scroll.

    Tilted, a tap picks whatever block you can see at that spot, so tapping the top of a wall digs that wall. If a wall stands in front of you, you show through it faintly. Your zoom and tilt are remembered in this browser.

    The other buttons

    • BUILD opens the sheet on the Build tab. See Buildings.
    • UPGRADE opens the sheet on the Upgrades tab. See Upgrades.
    • The layer button takes you between layers and says how: DIG DOWN digs a new hole where you stand, CLIMB DOWN goes down a hole that's already there, and underground DIG UP and CLIMB UP do the same through the ceiling. It dims where you can't. See The mine.

    Close the sheet with Done, or swipe it down by its handle.

    The screen

    There's no text to read in the game. Here's what everything you see means.

    Resource counters

    The four chips at the top are what you're carrying: 🪵 wood, 🪨 stone, ⛏️ ore and 💠 crystal. A green +n/s under a count is your income from buildings and the Abundance Engine, assuming you're there to pick it up.

    Depth marker

    Underground, a small ▼ n marker under the counters shows which layer you're on: 1 is just below the forest. Once you have been down, the depth gauge on the left edge shows the same as a column of layers (see The mine).

    On the map

    • Faint green outline: the tile Gather will work next.
    • Blue outlines (while the Build tab is open): where each kind of building would go.
    • A dial over a building means it holds goods waiting for you to collect. It fills green as they pile up and turns gold when it's full, and a full building stops working until you collect. Buildings whose goods Logistics hauls in as they're made show no dial.
    • Little sprites arcing away: what you just gathered, flying to its pile in your stockpile. When home is far, they fly off screen in its direction.
    • A golden burst of sparks, a fanfare and a Milestone banner under the counters naming it: you've reached a milestone.
    • Darkness after dusk, with a pool of lamplight around you. See The world.
    • Black: fog. You haven't been there yet.

    The sheet

    BUILD and UPGRADE open a sheet with the Upgrades and Build tabs, plus Beyond once you've mastered a world (see Beyond). Tap the map above it (or Done) to close it. That tap only closes the sheet and doesn't move or gather. Each price is a row of pills, one per resource. While you're short, a pill shows how much more of that resource you still need to gather (red), and a resource you already have enough of shows a tick. Once you have it all, the pills turn green and show what it costs. The amounts count down live as you gather. In Upgrades, each family has a row of dots: an empty dot hasn't been started, a tinted dot is affordable now, a half dot is part way, and a solid green dot is maxed.

    The bottom of the sheet holds Wiki (this page), Reset world (starts over, after asking), Sound and Textures, which shows the texture pack in use and loads your own (see Texture packs).

    Sound plays as soon as your browser lets a page make sound. Some allow it straight away. Most wait until you've touched or clicked the page once, and on an iPhone that has to be a tap, since a drag doesn't count. After that, sound comes back by itself when you return to the tab or a call ends.

    Performance panel

    A tool for tuning the game, kept out of the way while you play. Press F3 to see how well the game runs on your device, and again to hide it. On a phone, open the game with ?perf at the end of its address. Its left edge is green at 55 fps or more, yellow from 30, red below.

    If something breaks

    If the game hits an error it stops and shows a red The game broke panel saying what went wrong (the error, where in the code, the game version and your browser), with a Reload button. Errors from scripts that aren't the game, such as the ones Safari's Share menu runs, are ignored. If saving stops working (storage full or blocked), a message says so straight away.

    The world

    Endless in every direction, different every time, and it goes down as well as out.

    Layout

    Each world comes from a random seed and has no edges: keep walking and it keeps going. Resources are spread evenly everywhere with no richer zones far from the start, so every direction is worth exploring. You begin on the dry land nearest the world's centre, in a clearing of home ground kept for your stockpile.

    The world has layers. The forest you start in is the surface; the mine is a stack of layers directly beneath it, lined up tile for tile. See The mine.

    Forests

    Forests differ from region to region, like real ones: cold country grows dark pinewood (pines and spruce), wet mild country pale birch groves, warm wet country broad oak woods with the odd apple tree, warm dry country maple woods in autumn colours, and where they meet, mixed wood with a bit of everything. Within a forest the trees vary too. Every forest yields wood the same way; only the look changes. See them all under Everything in the game.

    Surface tiles

    Richness is how many swings a deposit takes before it's exhausted. Each tile rolls its own value in that range. Boulders, ore rocks and crystals shake and shed chips with every swing, and shrink as they run down.

    Fog

    The world you know is a plateau high over the dark. Everything within tiles of you is revealed as you walk, and stays revealed. The Cartographer perk widens this by 2.

    Where the known ground ends, it drops away as a cliff: its face shows the layers beneath that spot, fading into the dark about layers down. Rock you've seen on those layers shows in its own colours, layers you've been down to are dim earth, and the rest are dark bands. Explore further and the new ground covers the old cliff, so there's always an edge out ahead of you. Edges facing away from you catch a pale lip.

    Water

    You can swim anywhere there's water, at of your walking speed. You'll be riding a little boat. Nothing can be built on water.

    Regrowth

    Forest and stone grow back; ore and crystal never do. Every seconds, each depleted forest or stone tile within tiles of you has a chance to regain one point of richness, up to where it started. Tiles far from you don't regrow, so a cleared patch only comes back while you're nearby.

    Day and night

    A full day lasts seconds and a new world starts at noon. Days are bright and warm. After dusk the world fades into darkness and you take out your lantern: a pool of lamplight around you, so you can always keep working. By day the lantern stays packed away. Nothing else changes at night.

    Gathering

    Four resources, two tools, and deposits that run dry.

    Resources

    • 🪵 Wood from forest, and from fossils in the deep mine. Uses the Axe.
    • 🪨 Stone from stone deposits and stone seams. Uses the Pickaxe.
    • ⛏️ Ore from ore deposits and ore seams. Uses the Pickaxe. Never regrows on the surface.
    • 💠 Crystal from crystal deposits and, from layer 3 down, crystal seams. Uses the Pickaxe. Rare, and never regrows on the surface.

    Yield and richness

    Each swing gives you as much as your tool's level allows (1 at the start, up to 32), but always takes exactly one point of richness from the tile. Better tools don't use up deposits faster; they get more out of each one. A forest at zero richness turns into a stump until it regrows.

    See Upgrades for the full tool tables.

    Finding ore and crystal

    Ore and crystal show up as rocks flecked with orange, and as pale blue crystal clusters. They're scattered everywhere, but crystal is rare. If none is near, walk in a straight line; the fog hides a lot.

    The mine

    Solid rock under the forest, as deep as you care to dig.

    Holes: going down and back up

    Every way between layers is a hole you can see, so it's always clear where you went deeper and where you came back up.

    • DIG DOWN, in the forest, digs a hole at your feet and drops you into a one-tile chamber in the rock directly below. It works on open ground, not in water. A new hole takes work: you dig for seconds with the first Pickaxe (quicker with better ones: the time is divided by the square root of its level), while a ring fills around the button and the pit opens at your feet. Step off the spot and you stop. Standing on a hole that's already there, the button says CLIMB DOWN and you go at once.
    • Underground the button goes up: CLIMB UP under a hole in the ceiling (the one you came down, say), DIG UP anywhere else, which digs a new one through the ceiling where you stand (it takes the same work as digging down). You can't dig up through bedrock, or come up inside a mountain or into water.
    • Home ground is sealed: you can't dig down from it, or come up onto it from below, so the stockpile's ground stays whole. Deeper down, under home, the rock is like anywhere else.
    • A way between layers never shares a tile with a building: you can't dig down under one or up into one, and you can't build on a hole or under one.
    • Every hole has a ladder in it. From above, a hole is a dark pit in the floor with the ladder going down; tap it within reach to drop through. From below, it's a pool of light falling through the ceiling with the ladder standing in it, and a beam when the view is tilted. Where a hole has another hole above it (in a column dug straight up or down), you see both: the pit ringed with light pouring into it, and the ladder running on up into the beam.
    • Holes stacked on top of each other make a column: drop into the top one and you fall all the way to the bottom.
    • Going down, you see the trip: the layer you left slides up out of sight, the rock you pass goes by with the shaft and its ladder down the middle, and the new layer comes up from below (the other way when you climb). A long fall passes one slab of rock per layer.
    • Once you have been down, the depth gauge on the left edge shows the layers as a column: the forest on top, every layer you've been down to, a dashed one for deeper still, and yours lit. The marker under the counters gives your depth as a number.
    • You can't gather from or build on a tile with a hole in it.

    Seeing in the dark

    Each layer starts unexplored, just like the forest. Underground you see the rock within tiles of you, and it stays revealed; shafts are hidden until you get close. The mine is one mass of rock: rock you haven't seen is a dark block, rock you have seen is a shade darker than the floors you dug, and your tunnels are cut down into it. Digging carves space out of the rock; it never builds a wall.

    Digging

    Underground, everything is solid and you can only walk through tunnels you dig. The easy way is to walk into the rock: keep pushing the stick (or holding a direction key) against a wall for seconds and the tile in front of you is dug out, so you tunnel as you go. You can also dig a tile by tapping it within reach, or with a Trail across nearby rock. Bedrock never gives, and a shaft only opens to a deliberate tap. Holding Gather on its own never digs.

    Each dig clears one tile into tunnel. Seams pay out like surface deposits, using your Axe or Pickaxe level. Plain soil holds nothing: its dirt is tossed aside with a +0, and digging it only clears the way. Tunnels stay where you dug them, on every layer.

    What's down there

    Deeper layers hold more kinds of rock:

    Finds

    Every × area of each layer hides up to finds in the rock. They glint in the dark from up to tiles away, even through rock you haven't uncovered, so there's always something close to dig toward. One dig breaks a find open and pays its loot all at once: each amount below, times the layer number, times your Axe (wood) or Pickaxe (everything else) yield.

    Shafts

    Every × area of each layer hides exactly one shaft, a marked hatch at least two tiles in from the area's edge. Dig your way to it and dig it once to open it. Opening it doesn't drop you; when you're ready, tap the open shaft from within reach and you fall to the layer below, landing directly underneath it. Shafts are the only way deeper than you've been.

    Layers line up exactly: the tile at a given spot on layer 3 is directly below the same spot on layer 2 and on the forest.

    Rules underground

    • No buildings underground, but you can place torches (from the Build sheet, for wood each), one on the tunnel floor where you stand. A torch lights the tunnel around it for good, much further than your lantern reaches.
    • It's always dark down here. Your lantern lights only a small pool at your feet, so tunnels you dug a few steps back sink into the dark again. Torches keep their stretch lit, and daylight falls through the holes you came down.
    • Logistics only collects from buildings on the layer you're on, so your surface camps simply fill up while you're below.
    • Nothing regrows underground.
    • Day and night carry on as usual: light through a hole to the forest fades at night.

    Buildings

    Make the world work for you.

    Placing

    Open BUILD, stand next to the right kind of tile, and tap Place. The building goes on the nearest suitable free tile within reach; blue outlines on the map show where. Each building you already own of a kind makes the next one of that kind cost × more.

    Extractors

    Extractors stand on a matching deposit and produce its resource forever. They don't use the deposit up.

    Converters

    Converters make a resource out of nothing on any open land: grass or any surface deposit, but not water or mountain. Each needs its blueprint from the Unlocks family in Upgrades first.

    Output

    A building's real rate is its base rate multiplied by Overclock, its resource's refinery chain, the Abundance Engine and the Legacy perk. The Build tab shows the current figure for each.

    Goods pile up inside the building until it's full ("Holds" above), then it stops. Nothing is lost; it just waits.

    Collecting

    Stand within tiles of a building and press Gather (it reads COLLECT) or tap the building. You take every whole unit from every building in that range at once.

    Logistics

    Once you unlock Logistics, every building within tiles of you sends its goods straight into your packs as they're made. Walk away and they fill up again. The trick is to cluster buildings into a base and work from the middle of it.

    The stockpile

    Everything you own stands on the ground around home, and the more you have, the further it spreads.

    Every world starts you in a round clearing of home ground, packed earth where nothing grows, nothing can be gathered and no hole can be dug. It's where your stockpile is kept. It spirals out from the middle: the biggest piles in the middle, smaller ones further out. It's a picture of what's in your packs, not a store: nothing has to be carried there, you can walk straight through it, and spending something anywhere takes it off the piles at once. New piles grow out of the ground; piles that go vanish in a puff.

    Reading it

    Each power of ten has its own kind of pile, and each digit of what you own is how many of that kind there are. It reads like an abacus: 3,452 wood is 3 carts, 4 log piles, 5 bundles and 2 single logs. Ten of a pile become one of the next, so the yard grows with the number of digits, not the number itself, and a glance at the biggest piles tells you how rich you are.

    Piles of ten million (107) and more need room: each covers a square of ground, one tile wider for every further power of ten. A hundred billion of something is a district of its own. With enough, the stockpile covers the map.

    Where it goes

    • Only on ground you've explored; explore around home and the yard has more room.
    • Home ground first. Once that's full, piles spill onto open grass around it.
    • Never on anything you can gather (forest, stone, ore, crystal), so a pile never hides a resource.
    • Never on water, mountains, holes or buildings, and never more than tiles from home. If it runs out of room, the smallest piles wait for more ground.
    • The yard stays put while you gather. Piles that are still needed never move, new piles take the best free spot, and only the piles that go (spent, or ten merged into one bigger pile) disappear. About every minute and a half the yard tidies itself, biggest piles back in the middle.
    • Zoomed far out, each pile is a dot of its resource's colour.

    What every kind of pile looks like comes from the texture pack (the tiers slot of piles in Everything in the game).

    Upgrades

    Everything in the Upgrades tab, level by level.

    Tools

    Your Axe and Pickaxe set how much each swing yields. Boots set your speed.

    Overclock is the one tool that works on your buildings rather than on you. Each level doubles what every building makes (Lumber Camps, Quarries, Mines, Crystal Drills and converters), wherever they are, so it's the best upgrade once a few buildings stand. You also hear it: the hum of your buildings rises with each level.

    Unlocks and blueprints

    One-time purchases.

    Each resource has a chain of ten refinery levels. Every level multiplies all production of that resource, from extractors and converters alike, and the levels compound.

    Beyond

    Start over in a new world, a little stronger each time.

    Embarking

    Embarking is the end of a world, not a step along the way. The Beyond tab doesn't appear at all until you've mastered your world: every tool, every refinery level and every unlock bought, and the Abundance Engine at level . Then the Embark button lights up once you've also taken in resources in this world. Everything that reaches your packs counts: gathering, digging, collecting, Logistics and the Abundance trickle. Until then the Embark row shows how far along you are. After your first Embark, the Beyond tab stays so you can spend Insight on perks.

    Embarking throws you into a brand new random world. You lose the old world, your inventory, tools, buildings, unlocks, refineries, the Abundance Engine and your milestone progress. You keep your Insight, your perks and your lifetime total.

    Insight

    Each Embark pays out Insight based on everything you've ever gathered, across all worlds:

    Insight gained = floor(√(lifetime total ÷ 2,000))

    So 1,000,000 lifetime gives 22, and 4,000,000 gives 44. Insight is spent on perks and never resets. The requirement to embark only counts the current world, so you can't embark again right away on old progress.

    Perks

    Legacy multiplies all building output and the Abundance trickle by 1.25 per level, compounding. Memory, Heirloom and Cartographer take effect from the next world you start.

    Milestones

    The game quietly tracks these in order and celebrates each one with a burst of sparks, a fanfare and a banner naming it. It never tells you the next one. Spoilers, if you'd rather find your own way.

    Milestones are checked in order, so one only counts once everything before it is done. They start over when you embark.

    Everything in the game

    Every thing you can see, what it is and what it does. The pictures are the default texture pack, Lanternlight.

    The game decides what exists and what it does; a texture pack decides what it looks like. The code next to each name is the id a pack uses for it, and the slots are the pictures and colours a pack can give it. Slots in bold are filled by every pack (the default fills them when yours doesn't).

    Texture packs

    The game decides what exists and what it does. You decide what it looks like.

    A texture pack is a pack.json file plus pictures. It can change the picture or colour of anything listed in Everything in the game: every tile, tree, boulder, building, the wanderer and their boat, every button and icon, and the colours of the dark and the cliffs. A pack can change as much or as little as you like; anything it leaves out keeps the default look.

    Using a pack

    Open the sheet (BUILD or UPGRADE) and tap Textures at the bottom. Pick the pack's pack.json, either on its own (if its pictures are inside it) or together with its picture files. The game checks the whole pack first: if anything is wrong it isn't used, and a panel lists every problem, naming the thing and slot that broke. A pack that works is used straight away and remembered in this browser. Default textures (shown while your pack is in use) goes back to Lanternlight.

    Start from the default

    The quickest way to make a pack is to copy the default one and change it.

    That file has every picture written inside it as SVG markup, so it works on its own: edit it, change its name, and pick it in the game. The same pack as separate files is in the game's source, in packs/default/.

    pack.json

    {
  "format": 1,
  "name": "Autumn",
  "author": "you",
  "tile": 64,
  "looks": {
    "tile.grass":  { "color": "#b8873a", "top": ["grass-a.png", "grass-b.png"] },
    "tile.forest": { "stand": ["maple.svg", "birch.svg", null], "cut": "stump.svg" },
    "tile.water":  { "top": { "frames": ["water-1.png", "water-2.png"], "fps": 2 } },
    "character.wanderer": { "image": "fox.png" },
    "color.backdrop": { "color": "#0b0710" }
  }
}
    • format is , the pack format this version of the game reads.
    • name is shown on the Textures button. author is optional.
    • tile is how many pixels of your pictures make one tile (default ). A picture that many pixels wide is one tile wide on the map; twice as wide is two tiles.
    • pixelated: true keeps pixel art crisp instead of smoothing it when scaled.
    • looks maps thing ids to their slots.

    Slots

    • color: a colour like "#4a7a33". Used far zoomed out, in cliffs, for sparks, and wherever there's no picture.
    • top: the ground. Stretched over the whole top of the tile, and squashed as the view tilts, so draw it straight from above. Neighbouring tiles touch, so keep edges plain if you don't want a grid.
    • face: the front of a raised tile (mountains, walls in the mine, the shore above water), seen when the view is tilted. One picture is one layer of height and repeats down taller faces.
    • stand: what you gather from the tile, standing on it, like trees and boulders. Only tiles you can gather have it: nothing in the game is decoration you can't pick up, so a pack can't put props on grass, water or tunnels. Drawn upright with its bottom centre on the middle of the tile; it never squashes and may stick out over its neighbours. Stone, ore and crystal wear down as they're mined: drawn smaller the less is left, to half size before they run out.
    • cut: what stands on a gathering tile once it's worked out (a stump, pebbles).
    • image: buildings, the wanderer and the boat, drawn upright like stand, and shown in the sheet.
    • icon: a square picture for counters, buttons and the sheet.
    • tiers: the stockpile's piles, a list of pictures, smallest first: the first is one of the resource, the next ten, then a hundred, and so on. Past the end of your list the last picture stands in. Draw them standing, about one tile wide; from ten million up the game draws them across their square of ground.

    Pictures

    Each picture can be:

    • a path next to pack.json: .svg, .png, .webp, .jpg or .gif;
    • SVG markup written straight into the JSON, starting <svg (it needs width and height);
    • a data:image/ URL, or an https:// address.

    Where a slot takes a picture, it can also take:

    • a list of variants: each tile picks one, always the same one for the same spot. null in the list means nothing there, so ["oak.png", null, null, null] puts an oak on about a quarter of the forest;
    • an animation: { "frames": [...], "fps": 2 }. Ground animations start at a different frame on each tile so water shimmers. For the wanderer the first frame is standing still and the rest loop while walking.

    Draw standing things facing right; the game mirrors them. Leave the shadow out too: the game draws one under everything that stands.

    What the game draws itself

    Some things stay the same in every pack, because they tell you how the game works: shadows, day and night, the lamplight around you, target outlines and Trails, the dial over a building that fills as it holds output, the fading of cliffs into the dark, and the floating numbers. Their colours come from the pack where it makes sense (a resource's colour tints its sparks; the skylight's colour lights holes in the ceiling).

    When a pack is wrong

    Nothing is half-applied. The game lists every problem and keeps the look you had. Typical ones:

    • tile.gras: no such thing in the game: a typo in an id; check the list.
    • tile.water icon: Water has no "icon": that thing doesn't have that slot.
    • tile.grass top: "grass.png" was not among the files picked with the pack: pick the pictures too, or write them into the file.
    • character.boat image: the picture failed to load or decode: the file isn't a picture the browser can read.

    If the default pack itself is broken, the game stops and says what broke rather than drawing a half-empty world.

    Saving

    Automatic, and kept in your browser.

    • The game saves every few seconds, right after anything important, and when you leave the page.
    • Saves live in your browser's local storage on this site. They don't follow you to another browser or device, and a private or incognito window may forget them when it closes.
    • Clearing site data for the game deletes your save.
    • Two tabs, one world. Every tab on this site shares the one save. If another tab has saved since this one last did, this tab won't overwrite it. It pauses and asks which world to keep: the other tab's or its own, each with time played and amount gathered, and the one further along is marked. A tab you closed or left behind can't quietly roll your progress back.
    • Reset world at the bottom of the sheet starts a fresh world. It asks first, and it can't be undone.
    • While the game is in active development, a new version may not read older saves. If a save can't be read, the game tells you and starts a new world.

    None of this is in the game. These are possibilities, not promises. Some may be built, some changed beyond recognition, most never. Anything that ships moves off this page and into the rest of the wiki.
